SecurityFive layers between your passwords and everyone else

Hover or tap a layer. Everything is encrypted before it touches storage, and nothing leaves the phone unless you send it.

  1. Master passwordNever stored and never sent anywhere. Only you know it. If you forget it, nobody, including the developer, can recover the vault.
  2. Argon2id key derivation64 MB of memory, 3 passes and a random salt turn your password into the vault key, making guessing slow and costly.
  3. AES-256 encrypted vaultThe database is encrypted with SQLCipher. Entries, notes, history and attachments are all ciphertext at rest.
  4. Android KeystoreFingerprint unlock and saved sign-in tokens are protected by keys held in the phone's secure hardware or Keystore.
  5. Encrypted backupsA backup file is the vault in encrypted form. Google Drive, WebDAV and any file location only ever see ciphertext.

FeaturesEverything a vault should do.

No entry limit. Every feature below is included.

Encrypted vault

Logins, notes, files and images in one SQLCipher database. Unlimited entries.

Fingerprint unlock

The fingerprint prompt opens by itself on the lock screen. Falls back to your master password.

Extra fields

Second usernames, passwords, PINs and websites on any entry, each with its own label.

One-time codes

Built-in TOTP generator with a live countdown, so your 2FA codes sit next to the login.

Generator

Passwords, passphrases and PINs with presets and an entropy-based strength meter.

Statistics

See weak, reused and short passwords, plus rankings by category and field.

Categories & archive

Several categories per entry, favourites, search, sorting and an archive for old logins.

History & files

Old passwords are kept in the entry's history. Attach files and images up to 5 MB, stored encrypted.

Hard to leak

Screenshots blocked by default, clipboard marked sensitive and cleared, auto-lock and self-destruct.

Backup & syncYour data, safe in more than one place

Back up to your own Google Drive, keep an encrypted file anywhere, or sync to a server you control.

  • Google Drive backupSign in with a Google account already on your phone. CipherON creates a folder named CipherON in your Drive and uploads the encrypted backup. It can only see files it created itself.
  • Backup fileSave or share an encrypted .cphn file, or let CipherON write one to a folder of your choice whenever the vault locks.
  • WebDAV syncSync with Nextcloud, ownCloud or any WebDAV server. Conflicts are detected, never overwritten silently, and older versions can be restored.
  • New phone?Restore from a backup file or from Google Drive on the first-run screen, then enter your master password.

QuestionsGood to know

Do I need an account?

No. You create a master password on your phone and that is all. The optional Google Drive backup uses your own Google account, only if you choose to connect it.

Where is my data stored?

In an encrypted database inside the app on your phone. The developer runs no servers for CipherON and receives no data. Backups go only where you send them.

What if I forget my master password?

It cannot be recovered. The password is never stored, and the vault key is derived from it. Keep it safe, and keep an encrypted backup.

What can CipherON see in my Google Drive?

Only the folder and backup files it creates itself (the drive.file permission). It cannot read the rest of your Drive. You can remove access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Which phones does it run on?

Android 8.0 or newer.

How do I get the app?

CipherON is currently shared as a direct install file. Contact the developer through the GitHub profile in the footer.

Keep your passwords where you can see them.
